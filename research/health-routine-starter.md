# Personal health and routine starter

Status: starter guidance only; not a personalized medical or nutrition plan.

## What is known and unknown

This workspace does not yet have the user's age, medical history, injuries, current activity level, training experience, sleep schedule, dietary preferences, allergies, or specific fitness goal. Do not set a calorie target, weight target, supplement plan, or intense training prescription until those details are supplied. If there is a chronic condition, significant pain, recent surgery, pregnancy, or a long period of inactivity, seek clinician guidance before increasing exercise.

## Safe starting structure

- Start with comfortable walking or other moderate movement and build gradually toward the WHO adult guideline of 150–300 minutes per week; some activity is better than none.
- Include muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days each week, adjusted to ability and recovery. Stop for chest pain, faintness, unusual shortness of breath, or sharp pain and seek appropriate care.
- Use regular meals built around varied vegetables and fruit, pulses/beans, whole grains or millets, and suitable protein foods. Prefer minimally processed foods, drink to thirst and conditions, and avoid turning this into a restrictive diet.
- Track only a few useful signals initially: movement minutes, strength sessions, sleep window, energy, and any symptoms. Review weekly; adjust one thing at a time.

## Flexible starter week

This is a gradual example, not a prescription. If currently inactive, start with shorter, comfortable sessions and build up only when recovery feels normal. The WHO adult target is 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly plus muscle-strengthening work on at least two days; it is a destination, not a requirement to jump to immediately.

| Day | Starter activity |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Comfortable walk, 15–20 minutes |
| Tuesday | Easy full-body strength practice, 15–20 minutes; use light resistance or supported movements and stop well before strain |
| Wednesday | Comfortable walk, 15–20 minutes |
| Thursday | Rest, or gentle mobility if it feels good |
| Friday | Repeat the easy strength practice, 15–20 minutes |
| Saturday | Comfortable walk, 20–25 minutes |
| Sunday | Rest and note how the week felt |

For the strength days, use familiar, pain-free movements with support as needed. A trainer or clinician can help choose movements and technique once equipment, experience, and any injuries are known. Do not push through pain, dizziness, chest symptoms, or unusual breathlessness.

## Flexible Indian meal pattern

Use this as a menu framework, not a measured diet:

- **Breakfast:** idli with sambar; or oats/poha with vegetables and curd; add eggs or another protein food if that fits your diet.
- **Lunch:** rice or roti (include whole grains when practical), dal/beans, vegetables, and curd; choose egg, fish, chicken, paneer, tofu, or another suitable protein according to preference.
- **Snack if hungry:** fruit with roasted chana, peanuts, or plain curd.
- **Dinner:** a similar varied meal with vegetables, a grain or millet, and dal/beans or another suitable protein.

Prefer variety, vegetables and fruit, pulses/beans, whole grains, and minimally processed foods. Adapt choices for allergies, medical advice, budget, culture, and appetite. The ICMR–NIN guidance describes a healthy meal around vegetables, whole grains, pulses/beans, modest nuts/seeds, fruit, and plain curd/yogurt; it does not define this user's portions or energy needs.

## Simple weekly check-in

Record: movement minutes, strength sessions, sleep window, energy, and any symptoms. If the week feels manageable, add a small amount of comfortable movement the following week. If fatigue, pain, or symptoms increase, reduce activity and seek appropriate advice.

2. Age range and current weekly activity; gym/home equipment and available days/minutes.
3. Any health conditions, medications, injuries, movement restrictions, or clinician advice.

6. What should be tracked, and what should remain private.
